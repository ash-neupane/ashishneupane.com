Which summer 2027 days will be wedding saait?

The Nepal Panchanga Nirnayak Samiti will not publish the 2084 BS list until around April 2027. This forecasts it from astronomy — trained on six published years, scored once on two years it never saw. The single most consequential finding: Chaturmas begins 14 July 2027, so a wedding in your 15 May – 31 August window has to happen in the first 60 days of it.

Step 1 — how a saait is actually set

Four gates, then a moment

A saait is not a day, it is a moment. The committee first strikes out whole stretches of the year with deterministic astronomy, then hunts inside what remains for an instant when the Moon's mansion, the lunar day, the karana and the rising sign all agree. The gates are the part that moves the most dates.

  • Solar season. The Sun must stand in Mesha, Vrisha, Mithuna, Vrischika, Makara or Kumbha. Karka through Tula is Chaturmas territory; Dhanu and Meena are Kharmas.
  • Chaturmas. From Harishayani Ekadashi (Ashadha shukla 11) to Haribodhini Ekadashi (Kartika shukla 11), Vishnu sleeps and every sacrament pauses — about four months with no weddings at all.
  • Shukra asta. When Venus is lost in the Sun's glare it cannot bless a marriage. Fitted threshold: 6.5° elongation.
  • Guru asta. Same for Jupiter, at 11.5°. These two gates alone are why 2081 BS had only 17 saait days while 2083 had 41.
  • Then the moment: a vivaha nakshatra, a tithi that is not rikta or Purnima, no Vishti karana, and an auspicious lagna rising — all at once.
Step 2 — lining the published dates up with the sky

The Moon's cycle and its constellations do most of the work

Six published years, 2,192 days, 186 of them saait. Sorting those days by where the Moon was gives the model its whole signal — but only once you stop reading the panchanga at sunrise.

The finding that made the model work. At sunrise, only of published saait days carry one of the eleven classical vivaha nakshatras — which looked like the classical rule simply did not apply in Nepal. But of them carry one at some point during the day. The committee is picking the moment, not the date. Every feature in this model is therefore a duration across the day, measured sunrise to sunrise, not a value read at sunrise.

Appendix

Method, test results, and what would change the answer

A1 · Protocol

Six years of published lists, split strictly by year. Hyperparameters were searched only against the validation years using leave-one-year-out; the test years were scored exactly once, at the end, and never informed a single choice.

A5 · The fitted model

A logistic head over seven duration features, applied only to days that pass the gates. Bigger feature sets — per-nakshatra weights, per-tithi weights, weekday, solar month — all scored worse in cross-validation: with 186 positive examples the compact model is the robust one.

A6 · Limitations

  • 2079 BS is missing. Sources disagree on its total (40 vs 56) and none enumerates its Baishakh days, so the year was excluded rather than guessed at.
  • Six years is a small sample. 186 positive days total. This is why the winning model is deliberately small and why every gate was checked for perfect recall.
  • The lagna is approximated. The committee computes an exact lagna window with planetary aspects for each muhurta; this model only measures how long an auspicious sign is rising during wedding hours. That is the largest remaining gap.
  • Ranking beats identification. Precision at the true count is about 44% — roughly two of every five predicted days will be right. Useful for choosing a range; not a substitute for the published patro.
  • Personal charts are out of scope. The committee's list is universal; your own horoscopes may rule out specific dates on it.
